Research Question

Literature Review

Sadykova, K. (2024). "Public Trust in Government in Kazakhstan's Framework of Good Governance." Мемлекеттік басқару және мемлекеттік қызмет, №2 (89).
Reviews Kazakhstan's institutional trust problem through a "good governance" lens. Reports Bureau of National Statistics data showing trust in law enforcement agencies (police, prosecution, anti-corruption agency) rising from 2022 to 2023, but still low in absolute terms; also cites a 2022 Economic Research Institute finding that only 52.5% of respondents felt able to influence government actions. No regional breakdown.
OECD (2025). OECD Public Governance Scan of Kazakhstan. OECD Publishing.
Confirms Kazakhstan has run an official government-trust survey (Ministry of Information and Social Development) since 2022, and situates Kazakhstan against a 30-country OECD comparison. No regional breakdown.
Kazakhstan Institute for Strategic Studies (KazISS / KISI) — national trust surveys, kisi.kz, 2026.
Recurring national surveys (~8,000 respondents, CAPI, sampled across all 17 regions + Astana/Almaty/Shymkent) reporting 70–87% trust in the President in recent years. Sampling design covers every region, but published results report only national aggregates — the strongest candidate for a data-access request.
Bureau of National Statistics, Agency for Strategic Planning and Reforms. "The level of public confidence in law enforcement agencies and the judiciary." Oct–Nov 2023.
18,005 households, "in all regions of the republic." Regional breakdowns exist for crime statistics (Almaty highest) but not for trust — trust is reported only nationally.
Bertelsmann Stiftung (2026). BTI 2026 Country Report — Kazakhstan.
A well-established, independent transformation/governance index. Dates the unrest precisely (Jan 2–11, 2022) and attributes it to instability "especially in the socially unstable western regions of Kazakhstan" — the citable basis for this project's region list. Documents the crackdown (10,000+ detained in Jan 2022 alone). No trust data or regional breakdown — supports the mechanism and region selection, not the missing trust numbers.
06

Theory and Hypotheses

  • Independent variable: Region of residence — Almaty city and the other regions hit hardest by the unrest (Mangystau, Turkestan, Akmola) vs. less-affected regions, as a proxy for direct exposure. Grounded in BTI (2026), which attributes the unrest specifically to instability concentrated in these regions.
  • Dependent variable: Institutional trust in government (broadly, not limited to one agency).
  • Hypothesis: People in Almaty and the other hardest-hit regions will show a larger decrease in institutional trust from before January 2022 to now, compared to people in less-affected regions.
  • Causal mechanism: People closer to the unrest were more likely to witness, or be personally affected by, the government's violent response (mass arrests, documented use of force, 238 confirmed deaths). Witnessing the state use force against civilians directly undermines trust in the state — distinct from simply witnessing the initial unrest itself.
