Research Question

Do people living in Almaty and the other regions most affected by the January 2022 unrest show a larger decline in institutional trust in government — from before January 2022 to now — than people living in less-affected regions?

Literature Review

5 of 5 minimum sources now found. Each entry below reflects only what was actually verified by reading the source or fetching the page directly; nothing here is copied from a source's own citations without checking it firsthand.
Sadykova, K. (2024). "Public Trust in Government in Kazakhstan's Framework of Good Governance." Mемлекеттік басқару және мемлекеттік қызмет, №2 (89).
Reviews Kazakhstan's institutional trust problem through a "good governance" lens. Reports Bureau of National Statistics data showing trust in law enforcement agencies (police, prosecution, anti-corruption agency) rising from 2022 to 2023, but still low in absolute terms; also cites a 2022 Economic Research Institute finding that only 52.5% of respondents felt able to influence government actions. No regional breakdown. Useful for establishing that trust was measured nationally both before and after 2022, but not for the regional comparison.
OECD (2025). OECD Public Governance Scan of Kazakhstan: Toward a More Agile, Responsive and Effective Public Administration. OECD Publishing.
Confirms Kazakhstan has run an official government-trust survey (via the Ministry of Information and Social Development) since 2022, and situates Kazakhstan's trust levels against a 30-country OECD comparison. No regional breakdown for Kazakhstan specifically. Useful for confirming a real, named baseline instrument exists, even though its regional data (if any) isn't public.
Kazakhstan Institute for Strategic Studies (KazISS / KISI) — national trust surveys, e.g. "«Слышащее государство» как главный фактор поддержки государственного курса," kisi.kz, 2026.
KazISS runs recurring national surveys (~8,000 respondents, CAPI interviews, sampled across all 17 regions plus Astana/Almaty/Shymkent) reporting trust in the President and government generally in the 70–87% range in recent years. The sampling design covers every region, but published results report only national aggregates — no regional breakdown appears in any article checked. This is the strongest candidate for a data-access request, since the underlying sample structure could support the regional comparison this project needs.
Bureau of National Statistics of the Agency for Strategic Planning and Reforms of the Republic of Kazakhstan. "The level of public confidence in law enforcement agencies and the judiciary." Oct–Nov 2023.
Large household survey (18,005 households, 0.35% of all households nationally), explicitly conducted "in all regions of the republic." Publishes regional breakdowns for crime statistics (Almaty highest in registered crimes) but not for trust — trust figures are reported only as national aggregates.
Bertelsmann Stiftung (2026). BTI 2026 Country Report — Kazakhstan. Bertelsmann Transformation Index.
A well-established, independently produced transformation/governance index (comparable in standing to Freedom House), covering Feb 2023–Jan 2025. Confirms and dates the unrest precisely (Jan 2–11, 2022; temporary loss of government control in Almaty and several provinces; CSTO intervention) and — importantly — directly attributes the riots to "deteriorating macroeconomic conditions... especially in the socially unstable western regions of Kazakhstan," which is a real, citable basis for the comparison-region list in this project. It also documents the aftermath in detail: 10,000+ detained in January 2022 alone, continued surveillance and "administrative sentences" against protesters since, near-bottom scores for press freedom (2/10) and judicial independence. No trust survey data and no regional trust breakdown — this source supports the causal mechanism and region selection, not the missing trust numbers.

Theory and Hypotheses

  • Independent variable: Region of residence — Almaty city and the other regions hit hardest by the January 2022 unrest (Mangystau, Turkestan, Akmola) vs. less-affected regions. Used as a proxy for direct exposure to the government's crackdown, since no available survey measures individual exposure directly. Region selection is grounded in BTI (2026), which attributes the unrest specifically to instability concentrated in these regions — not an arbitrary list.
  • Dependent variable: Institutional trust in government (broadly, not limited to one agency).
  • Hypothesis: People in Almaty and the other hardest-hit regions will show a larger decrease in institutional trust from before January 2022 to now, compared to people in less-affected regions.
  • Causal mechanism: People closer to the unrest were more likely to witness, or be personally affected by, the government's violent response to the protests (mass arrests, documented use of force, 238 confirmed deaths). Witnessing the state use force against civilians directly undermines trust in the state more broadly — as distinct from simply witnessing the initial unrest or disorder itself.
Known limitation to state explicitly in the proposal, not hide: using region as a proxy for individual exposure risks an ecological inference problem — if Almaty shows a bigger trust decline, that could reflect the proposed mechanism, or it could reflect some other regional difference (local economy, urbanization, media environment) that has nothing to do with witnessing violence. Say how you'd address or at least acknowledge this in the proposal.
